# Interview questions

Ask 10 to 15 of these, one at a time, in plain words. Skip what the job description already answers. Follow up on vague answers with one short question ("Can you give me an example?"). Each topic notes what the answers feed.

## The rhythm of the week

Feeds: the role card, tool rhythms, and the timing of reminders.

- What does a normal week look like for you? What happens every week without fail?
- What comes up once a month or once a quarter, and when is it due?
- Which deadline would hurt most to miss?

## Who reads your work and what they need

Feeds: the role card (audiences), output formats, and number sign-off.

- Who reads what you produce? Leaders, partners, your team, customers?
- What does each of them need from you, and in what shape: a slide, a short note, a table?
- Which of your numbers go to leaders or onto published pages?

## Where your time goes

Feeds: the ranked list of first automations.

- What takes the most time each week?
- What do you do by hand that feels like it should be automatic?
- What do you put off because it is tedious?

## What each recurring task starts from

Feeds: the sources list, the manual steps for each tool, and recipe choice.

- For your main recurring task, what do you start from: a spreadsheet, a document, an export, an email, a meeting?
- Where does that live, and how do you get a fresh copy?
- Which documents are the official source of truth for dates, numbers, or policy?

## What must never go wrong

Feeds: the role card, input checks, and learned rules.

- What mistake would be most embarrassing or costly?
- Are there words, names, or numbers that must always be written a certain way?
- How do you check your own work today before it goes out?

## Confidential data

Feeds: the confidential flag on tools and what stays out of searches and version history.

- What in your work is confidential? For example unannounced offerings, partner names, pricing, or people matters.
- Is there anything I should never put in a search or a draft?

## Tools you inherited

Feeds: adopted tool records and the backup guide.

- Do you look after anything someone else built: a spreadsheet with formulas, a small program, a web page, a scheduled report?
- For each: where does it live, how do you run it, and when did it last work?
- Who could help if it broke?
